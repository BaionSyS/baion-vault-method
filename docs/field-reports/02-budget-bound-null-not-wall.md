# Field report 02 — A budget-bound null mislabeled as a wall

## Initial claim

A chain of experiment summaries described repeated non-attainment as a hard capability or search wall. The label began propagating across several versions even though the runs had all stopped at the same finite resource budget.

## Missing gate

The evidence established only that the target was not reached within the tested budget. Most runs were still improving when the cap arrived, so the result had not separated a geometry-bound limitation from a budget-bound search failure.

The word “wall” was stronger than the receipt.

## Catch

The claim was quarantined and a discriminator was run at a substantially larger budget. A valid run reached the target after passing the old stopping point. That constructive result falsified the hard-wall interpretation for the tested case.

## Correction

The wall language was retracted across the prior chain. Every prior version remained preserved and hash-identifiable. The corrected state said that the earlier nulls were budget-bound under their tested conditions; broader behavior across the full run distribution remained open.

## Enforcement added

- Separate “not reached under this budget” from “unreachable.”
- Inspect convergence state at the stopping boundary before naming a wall.
- Require a discriminator that can distinguish budget limitation from capability limitation.
- Let a constructive in-bounds witness settle reachability within its scope.
- Preserve every prior wall claim and connect it to the correction rather than silently rewriting the chain.

## What the gate saved

The quarantine prevented a provisional machine-generated verdict from becoming architectural canon and kept the next experiment aimed at the actual uncertainty instead of defending a false limitation.
