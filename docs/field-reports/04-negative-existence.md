# Field report 04 — An absence claim skipped the authoritative search

## Initial claim

A strategy was built around the belief that no member of a target class operated a particular type of program. The apparent absence looked like a meaningful opportunity.

## Missing gate

An upstream synthesis named the authoritative registry in its sources but did not record a query that could have found counterexamples. Later AI passes inherited the framing and produced conflicting secondary details without resolving the primary-source question.

## Propagation

The unsupported absence claim crossed into targeting, partnership assumptions, and follow-on research. One highly ranked target was itself a counterexample to the claim.

## Catch

A direct search of the authoritative registry found multiple current counterexamples. The claimed gap did not exist within the stated boundary.

## Correction

The absence claim and dependent strategy were withdrawn. The historical chain remained available so the propagation path could be examined instead of erased.

## Enforcement added

- “No X exists” requires a positive search that could have found X.
- The receipt records the authority, procedure or query, date, and search boundary.
- Naming a registry in a bibliography is not evidence that it was queried.
- A later AI answer is not automatically more reliable; authority and procedure must be inspectable.

## What the gate saved

The primary-source check prevented outreach and resource allocation based on a problem the target community did not have in the claimed form.
